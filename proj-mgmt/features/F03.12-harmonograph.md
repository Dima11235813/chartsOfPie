# F03.12 — Harmonograph

Consonance you can see. The current interval becomes a Lissajous figure: the lower note drives x
and the higher note drives y.

- [x] Interval = the lowest two notes sounding together, else the last two melody notes
- [x] Caption with notes, interval name, the equal-tempered ratio, the nearest pure ratio and
      the difference in cents, e.g. `A4 + C5 · minor 3rd · ratio 1.1892 ≈ 6:5 (−15.6 cents)`
- [x] **Pure ratios** toggle swaps in the just ratio. Pure figures retrace themselves crisply
      (enough cycles to close: q periods for p:q); tempered ones drift and slowly turn.
- [x] Gradient between the two notes' digit colours; gentle decay; reduced motion stops the turning
- [x] `viz/harmonograph.ts` unit-tested: A4 = 440 Hz, tempered fifth −1.955 cents, major third
      +13.686 cents, compound intervals

Lesson hook (E04): why equal temperament is a compromise, and what "beating" looks like.
